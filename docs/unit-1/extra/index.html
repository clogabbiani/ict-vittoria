Unit 1 · Extra

Extra: codes, Enigma and Moore's law

Secret codes turn letters into other letters, and machines can do it much faster than people. Try two of them, then see how fast chips grew.

Not part of the test. For anyone who wants more after lesson 1.

01The Caesar wheel

More than 2,000 years ago, Julius Caesar hid his messages by moving each letter three places in the alphabet: A becomes D, B becomes E. It is easy to use, and also easy to break: there are only 25 keys to try.

02A mini Enigma

Enigma was a coding machine used in the Second World War. Inside, wheels called rotors changed each letter, and they moved after every key press. To read a message you needed the same machine with the wheels in the same starting position.

03Moore's law

In 1965 Gordon Moore, a chip engineer, predicted that the number of transistors on one chip would keep doubling; in 1975 he said about every two years. It kept happening for about 50 years: from about 2,300 in the Intel 4004 (1971) to billions in the chip of your phone.
